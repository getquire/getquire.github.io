About

We're building the
tool we kept faking.

Before Margin, the three of us spent our weekends stitching together scripts that dumped a company's documents into a model and hoped. It worked about 70% of the time, which is another way of saying it didn't work.

The origin

A wrong number in a board deck.

In 2024, our co-founder Rita shipped a board deck with a retention figure pulled from an internal wiki page that had been superseded four months earlier. Nobody caught it for a quarter.

The wiki page wasn't wrong when it was written. It was wrong when it was read, and there was no way to tell the difference. That gap — between what a document said and whether it still held — is the whole company.

Every AI tool we tried made it worse, not better. They were extremely good at producing a fluent paragraph from a stale page and no good at all at telling you the page was stale.

"Where did this come from?"

If a tool can't answer that question in one click, it isn't finished. That's the whole product thesis, and it's why we shipped citations before we shipped a chat box.

What we believe

Five positions we'd defend in public.

01 — A citation is a feature, not a footnote

An answer you can't verify is a rumor with good grammar. We would rather ship a slower answer that survives scrutiny than a fast one that doesn't.

02 — "I don't know" is a valid output

Refusal is a capability, not a failure. Most of our evaluation budget goes into teaching the system when to decline, and it's the hardest thing we do.

03 — Retrieval is the product

Models improve on someone else's schedule. Parsing, permissions and ranking improve on ours. We invest where our effort compounds.

04 — Your documents are not our training data

Not now, not under a future policy update, not in aggregate, not "anonymized". It's in the contract because we intend to be held to it.

05 — Automation earns trust, it doesn't assume it

Agents ask permission before they act outside your workspace until you tell them to stop asking. Nobody should discover what our software did from a customer.

And one we've changed our mind on

We launched with a chat interface because everyone had one. It was wrong. People don't want a conversation, they want a document that answers back — so we rebuilt around the notebook in 2025.
