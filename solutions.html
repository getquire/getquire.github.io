Research & strategy

Stop rebuilding the same landscape every quarter.

Your last three market reviews already contain most of the answer. Margin treats them as the corpus and only goes looking for what changed.

  • Week one: ask across every past deck, call transcript and interview note at once
  • Month three: a Monday agent that posts a diff — what moved since the last brief, with sources
  • Interview synthesis: 40 transcripts to a themed summary with quote-level attribution
  • Automatic contradiction flags when two analysts wrote incompatible things

Typical result

Two-hour synthesis meeting → a 7am brief

Northbeam's research team replaced their weekly readout with a generated brief and kept the meeting only for disagreements. Twelve hours a week back across the team.

Legal & compliance

Review the exceptions, not the boilerplate.

Margin compares an incoming document against your own executed agreements and your playbook — not against a generic notion of what a contract should say.

  • Clause-level extraction across your whole executed-contract archive
  • "Show me every MSA with uncapped liability" answered in one query
  • Obligation calendars pulled out of signed agreements automatically
  • Reviewer trail that survives an audit: who asked, what was retrieved, what was produced
Support & success

Answers that age with your product.

Macros go stale the day after they're written. Margin drafts from the current docs, the current changelog and the last thirty resolved tickets on the same topic.

  • Draft replies with the source doc attached, so the agent can verify before sending
  • Deflection candidates: questions asked 40 times that have no doc page yet
  • Escalation summaries that include what was already tried
  • A weekly "what broke" digest built from tickets, not from vibes

Typical result

First-response time down 61%

Pact's support team keeps a human on every reply but stopped writing them from scratch. The measurable win was consistency, not speed — new hires answer like veterans in week one.

Product & engineering

Recover the reasoning, not just the outcome.

The code tells you what was built. Margin tells you what was rejected and why — which is the part that stops a team relitigating a decision every eight months.

  • Feedback synthesis across tickets, calls, reviews and sales notes
  • RFC context on demand: "what did we decide about multi-region and when?"
  • Onboarding paths that answer a new engineer's questions from real history
  • Release notes drafted from merged PRs and the tickets they closed
Finance & ops

Where did that number come from?

Board decks are assembled from a dozen exports and someone's memory. Margin keeps the provenance attached so the follow-up question has an answer.

  • Trace any figure in a deck back to the export and the query that produced it
  • Variance narratives drafted from the actuals plus last quarter's commentary
  • Vendor spend questions answered across invoices, contracts and approvals
  • Close checklists that flag the documents that didn't arrive

Typical result

Board prep from three days to four hours

Not because the deck writes itself, but because the fact-checking pass stops being a scavenger hunt.
