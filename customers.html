Northbeam · Research · 240 people

They deleted a recurring meeting.

Northbeam's research team spent every Monday morning synthesizing what had changed across their market. Twelve analysts, two hours, every week.

Quire indexed six years of decks, call transcripts and analyst notes. An agent now posts a diff brief at 07:00 — what moved, what contradicts last week, and where the sources disagree. The meeting became a thread.

The part that didn't work: the first version of the brief was too long and nobody read it. It took four iterations and a hard 400-word cap before it stuck.

Halden · Legal · 900 people

First-pass redline in 90 seconds.

Halden signs about 60 inbound agreements a month. Every one used to get a full read by a lawyer billing at a rate that made the exercise absurd.

Quire indexed 4,000 executed contracts plus their negotiation playbook. An agent redlines on upload, marks deviations by clause, ranks by risk exposure and attaches the closest precedent from their own archive.

The part that didn't work: it over-flagged for the first six weeks — 22 issues per contract, most trivial. They tuned the risk threshold with their own historical outcomes and it settled around seven.

Pact · Support · 60 people

New hires answer like veterans.

Pact's problem wasn't speed, it was variance. The same question got three different answers depending on who picked up the ticket.

Quire drafts from the current docs, the changelog and the last thirty resolved tickets on the topic — with the source attached so the agent verifies before sending. Humans still send every reply.

The part that didn't work: they tried full auto-send for tier-one tickets in month two. It was fine 96% of the time, and the 4% was bad enough that they turned it off and haven't turned it back on.

Orrery · Product · 420 people

A decision log nobody had to write.

Orrery kept relitigating architecture decisions because the reasoning lived in threads, not documents. Every eight months the same debate reopened.

Quire reconstructed six major decisions from RFCs, Slack, meeting recordings and PR discussions — with the rejected alternatives and who objected. It's now the first thing new staff engineers read.

The part that didn't work: two of the six reconstructions were wrong on the "why" because the real reason was never written anywhere. Quire flagged both as low-confidence, which is the only reason they got caught.

The two who left

Both said the same thing: their content was too thin.

One was a 15-person startup whose entire knowledge base was four Notion pages — Quire had nothing to retrieve, and a model without a corpus is just a chatbot with extra steps. The other had rich content locked in a system we didn't have a connector for and couldn't justify building one for a single customer.
