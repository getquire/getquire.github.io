The problem

Your company already wrote the answer. Nobody can find it.

The average knowledge worker spends 9.3 hours a week hunting for context that already exists — in a slide deck from last quarter, a Slack thread nobody archived, a PDF in someone's downloads folder.

Search finds documents

You wanted an answer. You got 40 tabs and a reading assignment.

Chatbots invent them

Fluent, confident, unsourced. Fine for brainstorming. Not fine for a board memo.

Nobody trusts either

About this site

None of this is real. The code is.

Margin is an invented company. This site is a design and copywriting exercise, open sourced under MIT so you can take the whole thing and ship your own.

What's fabricated

The company, the product, the four case studies, every quoted person, every metric — the 9.3 hours, the 118% retention, the 94% refusal rate, the benchmark tables. The SOC 2 and ISO badges. The funding round. The job listings and their salary bands.

All of it was written for this exercise. None of it is a claim about any real organization.

What's real

The design system, the markup, and the page structure. The visual language follows a published style spec — a "soft daylight notebook" aesthetic — applied as written: #ebf5ff canvas, #fafdff cards, charcoal pills as the only dense element, display type at weight 500 only, 32px card radius, no shadows on content.

The spec supplied tokens only. No copy was taken from it or from the site it describes.

How it's built

Static HTML. One stylesheet. About 30 lines of JavaScript for the mobile nav, the FAQ accordion and scroll reveals. No framework, no build step, no dependencies.

Seventeen pages, including a 404 styled as the product's own refusal state. Geist from Google Fonts; Inter Tight stands in for the commercial display face.

Take it

MIT licensed. Rename the brand, replace the copy, keep the system. If you reuse the structure, delete the fabricated trust signals first — the certification badges, the customer quotes, the benchmark numbers. Shipping those as real claims about your own company would be a lie with good typography.
